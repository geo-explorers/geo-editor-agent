# Debate claim curation — working procedure
# Debate claim curation — working procedure   (id=3dd273e214eb818a9c06d4a766982463)
last_edited: 2026-09-16T21:36:00.000Z

Working procedure · 16 September 2026 · Noesis / Agent flows. Use when curating claims around a public debate, reviewing an existing collection, or proposing missing debate claims. This is a documented procedure being developed toward a reusable skill; it is not an installed publishing skill.
# Purpose
## Curate debates worth having
A collection should help society resolve a live, consequential disagreement. Start with the practical decision people need to make now: what should change if they accept or reject the claim? Prioritize positions prominent in current public, political, expert or institutional discourse, including settled policies that serious participants are actively challenging. Simple, direct wording can carry a substantial intellectual debate. Relevance and technical plausibility alone are not enough.
### Required result
Broad, substantial positions come first. Coherent narrower debates follow. Specific predictions, incidents and technical disputes belong near the bottom; low-interest or weak claims come last. Keep the public page focused on the claims, with provenance and editorial reasoning in a linked review record.
# Procedure
## 1. Understand the task and feedback
Read the current page, every available comment and its exact anchor, and the current mirror records. Preserve editor additions. Record each comment as an issue, an accepted correction or reasoned disagreement, the affected IDs, and a concrete disposition. Reconcile missing comment counts rather than treating a preview as the full review.
Separate task scope from publishing authority. A request for a new Notion version does not authorize editing Geo, mirror source fields, votes, or existing published collections.
## 2. Map the live debate, then find or propose its claims
First outline the decisions each collection needs to expose: who acts, what action or judgment is disputed, why it matters now, and what is genuinely unresolved. For a current-discourse task, consult recent primary statements and credible reporting within the requested time window. Record dates and provenance in the review record; do not mistake an editorial framing for a speaker's exact position or treat a source link as proof of popularity.
The existing database is a source of candidates, not the boundary of the debate. Search for claims that fit the important decision rather than changing the collection's purpose to accommodate the nearest available row. A prominent, important missing position should be proposed even if the mirror has no exact equivalent.
Use the agreed mirrors or other authorized sources. A complete same-session snapshot may supply the candidate roster; re-fetch selected and disputed records. State actual coverage and cutoff. Search beyond the previous selected subset when rebuilding.
For each plausible candidate, read its original wording, source references and relevant editorial context. Identify its actor, commitment, modality, scope, timeframe, conditions and comparator. Do not infer truth, speaker endorsement or a serious dispute merely from a source link, tag, popularity score or a prominent speaker.
## 3. Apply the debate-value test
### Ask these questions before inclusion
- What important decision or reconsideration would agreement or disagreement help resolve now? Is the proposition practical, recognizable and genuinely contested, rather than a broad aspiration almost everyone can accept?
- What is the strongest reasonable argument for and against the actual proposition?
- Does available context show a real public, expert, policy or intellectual debate, or am I inventing a second side?
- Does the disagreement have meaningful stakes and a clear commitment?
- Is this a substantive proposition, or merely a report of what someone said, a definition, generic possibility, truism or vague complaint?
Record the judgment and evidence privately. Distinguish a reasoned possible objection from a sourced participant position. Serious arguments matter more than prestige; a minority position can qualify. Never manufacture false balance around established facts or pretend equal evidence on both sides.
### Decide the disposition
Keep: substantive, relevant debate. Move lower: useful but narrow. Context only: evidence/report rather than a good voting proposition. Omit as redundant: another wording or reverse of the same voting issue. Review at bottom: weak interest or unclear commitment. Proposed to remove: specific reason the original is unsuitable for the debate product. Removal is a review proposal, not deletion.
Low relevance to this question, poor wording, lack of a listed source, and low debate value are different defects. Do not merge these judgments or delete a useful minority position just because it is unpopular.
## 4. Organize by breadth and one question
### Page order
- Broadest positions on the central question and broad policy choices.
- Substantial narrower questions and mechanisms.
- Specific implementation choices, time-bound predictions, named models or incidents.
- Low-interest review, then Proposed to remove at the bottom. Proposed claims and proposed alternatives belong inside their relevant substantive collections.
Aim for two to four substantive claims per collection, each at a comparable level of breadth, relevance and quality. Give readers several distinct commitments within one coherent issue; one proposition and its exact opposite are the same voting question and count as one. Broad-to-specific progression primarily happens between collections, rather than using narrow details to fill a broad collection.
If a collection has only one claim, search for qualified peers, propose a genuinely important missing position supported by the debate agenda, or regroup it with a genuinely compatible collection. Do not meet the count by adding weaker claims, irrelevant material, narrower claims, paraphrases or negations. Keep a singleton only as a reasoned exception when no sound grouping is available. The two-to-four preference applies to substantive debate collections, not administrative tables of proposed wording or review decisions.
Do not mix risk scenarios with incentives or remedies just because all concern AI. Loss of control, misuse, competitive incentives, and evaluation policy are separate questions. Omit empty categories.
Use compact question or issue headings. Avoid pro/anti buckets that treat every relevant claim as direct support or opposition. A loss-of-control risk is not a prediction of extinction; a treaty is not a transnational government; a liability recommendation is not proof that current law is sufficient.
## 5. Reuse the debate before proposing a new claim
### Mandatory novelty check
Search the full available roster for exact, synonymous, broader/narrower and opposite formulations. Record the nearest existing IDs and the precise new commitment, if any.
A claim already lets readers agree and disagree. Do not create its negation just to represent the other side. Existing opposing entities may carry distinct histories, but they need not both occupy a curated collection; presentation deduplication does not authorize graph merging.
Propose an important missing debate position directly in the collection where people should encounter it. If an existing claim is only an approximate or weak fit, propose a clearer alternative there and show the closest existing claims beside it. State in the review record whether it is a same-meaning rename, a changed-scope alternative, or a genuinely new commitment. Do not retain both a weak original and its near-equivalent alternative as separate debate rows merely to increase the count.
Do not fill a section to meet a numeric target. A missing factual detail is not automatically a useful debate claim, but missing coverage of a practical, prominent decision is a reason to draft one. Zero new proposals is valid only when the important debate positions are already adequately covered.
### Writing test
Use a short, natural, headline-like proposition that makes one clear commitment. Preserve substantive qualifications; “catchy” does not authorize exaggeration. Read it aloud and remove jargon and padding.
Do not convert “in the next three years” into an arbitrary calendar day. Record the reference date in the review metadata when useful. Preserve dates in existing claim wording; do not silently rewrite them.
## 6. Keep kinds of change separate
Distinguish existing claim, proposed rename, genuinely new claim, collection placement, context omission, and proposed removal. Preserve original IDs and source wording. A clearer formulation of the same idea is a rename candidate, not a new claim.
Meaning-changing edits require a new proposition and fresh position decisions. Do not transfer existing votes to altered meaning. In a Notion-only task, queue Geo wording requests visibly and report that they remain unapplied.
## 7. Build and review the whole page
Use the same three-column table in every collection: Claim | Geo URL / status | Closest existing claims. Existing rows keep their original wording and Geo URL. Proposed rows state the proposed wording in the Claim cell, show PROPOSED in capitals in the URL/status cell, and link the nearest existing database claims in the third cell. Use one to three genuinely close matches; mark no close match found when that is the actual search result. A nearest match is not automatically equivalent or an approved merge target.
Place proposed new claims and better wording alternatives directly in the substantive collection. Keep original-versus-proposed meaning, supporting sources, uncertainty and publication disposition in the linked review record. Do not segregate proposals at the bottom or create a competing second row for the same voting question. Keep the page focused on claims and the requested comparison links.
### Editorial acceptance checks
- The first collection contains the broadest debate, without narrow dates, named incidents or technical details.
- Each substantive collection normally contains two to four distinct claims of comparable breadth, relevance and quality; singleton exceptions have a reason. Each collection addresses one coherent issue, and specificity increases between collections down the page.
- Every main claim passes the serious-disagreement test; factual context and weak voting prompts are handled separately.
- Important current decisions are covered, including inline proposals for genuine gaps; the page is not constrained by the database's present wording.
- No new-entity proposal merely repeats or reverses an existing proposition. Same-meaning wording improvements are marked as alternatives for review, with the original linked as the closest claim rather than duplicated as a second debate row.
- Every proposed row is visibly marked PROPOSED, lives in the appropriate collection and links its closest existing claims; a Geo URL is never fabricated for a draft.
- Proposed wording is short, natural and meaning-preserving where labeled a rename.
- Every editor comment maps to an applied correction or a visible scoped exception.
- Every selected row and link is read back; counts distinguish claims, placements, proposals and removal candidates.
Verify semantics and structure separately. Correct links and successful saves do not establish a good collection. Record remaining disagreement honestly and use an independent reviewer only when that review is assigned.
# Feedback and future skill
## Regression examples from this task
### Failures this procedure must catch
- An attributed opinion accepted because its mirror says source checking is not applicable → independently verify attribution against the original passage. Preserve uncertainty, conditions and the claimed outcome; research progress is not a guarantee of solving a safety problem.
- Several technical research benefits used as the whole slowdown debate → select distinct consequential tradeoffs, then use technical details as supporting arguments. Record whether each proposal is the speaker’s position, a critic’s position or an editorial inference.
- A broad AI-pause debate filled with an AGI-only claim → preserve the scope difference and propose the broader missing position inline.
- An easy-to-agree aspiration such as “global rules are needed” → find the contested decision about authority, strength, feasibility or enforcement.
- “AGI should not have one controller” used for public decision-making → distinguish ownership concentration from who legitimately makes societal choices.
- A useful missing position or clearer alternative hidden at the bottom → place it in the collection, mark PROPOSED and link its closest existing claims.
- Attractive collections built only from available rows → check the live debate agenda for important missing practical decisions.
- “Threat by 2030” in a broad-positions collection → move to specific timelines.
- “Loss of control, misuse and competitive pressure” as one bucket → separate the questions.
- “Technology can be weaponized” as a headline debate → propose removal from debate selection, preserving useful evidence elsewhere.
- A September 16 cutoff manufactured from the task date → use natural timing, or reuse the existing timeline claim.
- A negative and affirmative version of one issue → one voting proposition, not two manufactured claims.
- A longer technical restatement of a known claim → reject the duplicate.
- A Geo rename requested in feedback during a Notion-only revision → record original ID and proposed wording; do not silently publish.
